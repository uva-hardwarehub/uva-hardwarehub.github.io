Semiconductors and AI Hardware

Hardware for AI, from atoms to the edge.

We connect UVA researchers who build the hardware AI runs on: new materials and devices, ultra-low-power circuits, wireless and photonic links, compute architectures, and trustworthy hardware/software systems. Our focus is energy-efficient, secure AI in the datacenter and at the far edge, including batteryless sensors that connect AI to the physical world.

About the hub

AI's progress is now limited by hardware: by the energy it takes to compute and move data, by the trustworthiness of the chips it runs on, and by how much of the physical world it can sense. The Semiconductors and AI Hardware Hub, one of the University of Virginia's Research Hubs, brings together researchers who work on these limits at every level of the stack.

Five research themes run from materials to systems. Three testbed themes put that hardware to work in health and wearables, in the built environment, and in autonomy, the last together with UVA's Physical AI Hub. Photonics, detector and materials work is coordinated with the Quantum Photonics, Materials and Algorithms Hub. The hub builds teams for major federal and industry programs, connects faculty with Virginia's VAST, VFAB and VMEC networks, and trains the next generation of chip designers.

Why now
  • Energy. The cost of computing and moving data now limits how far AI can scale.
  • Data. Frontier models trained on internet data. The next gains need continuous, grounded data from the physical world.
  • Trust. AI in critical systems needs hardware that is secure and verifiable from the ground up.
  • Workforce. National semiconductor priorities (CHIPS, DoD microelectronics) need chip designers.

Impact

Putting AI to work in the real world

AI's next leap will not come from software alone. AI is running into physical limits: the power it draws, the reach of its sensors into the real world, and the trust we can place in the chips beneath it. Every one of these is a hardware problem. The hub brings together the researchers who solve them and turns that work into outcomes in health, security, infrastructure and the economy.

EnergyAI's power demand is outpacing the grid that feeds it.
ReachAI can't act on what it can't sense, and most of the world has no sensors.
TrustDefense, health and infrastructure need hardware that is secure by design.
TalentThe nation needs a new generation of chip designers.

Research themes

Five themes follow the hardware stack from materials to systems. Three testbeds show that hardware working in real settings.

Core hardware stack

T1

Materials and Beyond-CMOS Devices

2D, ferroelectric and wide-bandgap materials; oscillator and Ising devices; spintronics; thermal transport; flexible and thin-film sensors.

T2

Ultra-Low-Power and Self-Powered Edge Systems

Nanowatt circuits and SoCs, energy harvesting, batteryless sensor nodes, energy-adaptive operation, analog/mixed-signal front ends.

T3

Wireless, mmWave/THz and Photonic Links and Sensing

Wakeup and ultra-low-power radios, mmWave/THz circuits and MEMS, integrated and microwave photonics, photodetectors, wireless sensing.

T4

AI Compute Architectures and Memory

Compute- and processing-in-memory, neuromorphic accelerators, GPU and memory systems, architectures under power and thermal limits, chiplets and packaging.

T5

Trustworthy HW/SW Co-design and Edge Intelligence

Secure architectures and compilers, on-device LLMs, trustworthy, private and efficient ML, federated and distributed learning at the edge.

Application and testbed themes

A1

Health, Wearables and Human Performance

E-textiles and bioelectronics, wearable and in-home physiological sensing, athlete monitoring, concussion and rehabilitation.

A2

Sensing the Built Environment

Sensors in materials, furnishings and landscapes; responsive, cyber-physical architecture; smart-city and occupancy sensing.

A3

Autonomy and Physical AI Interface

Low-power perception and compute for robots and vehicles; assurance of AI-enabled cyber-physical systems. Shared with the Physical AI Hub.

Signature initiative

The Sensory and Computational Fabric for Physical AI

Imagine energy-autonomous nodes built into textiles, bodies, structures and machines. They sense with new materials and devices, compute and learn in nanowatt, in-memory hardware, and link over ultra-low-power radio and photonic channels, with security built in. Together they act as the senses of large AI models: they filter, compress and learn locally, and send grounded signal upstream.

The initiative draws on every research theme and is demonstrated in all three testbeds: chiplet networks woven into fabric for health and sport, instrumented rooms and landscapes, and low-power perception for autonomous systems.

  • T5Security built into the hardware; learning split across nodes
  • T4In-memory, neuromorphic and chiplet-based AI compute
  • T3Wakeup radios, mmWave/THz sensing and photonic links
  • T2Nanowatt compute and energy harvesting
  • T1New sensing materials, ferroelectric and beyond-CMOS devices

Why UVA

Proven leadership across the stack, from materials and devices to photonics, compute and systems.

Self-powered sensor nodesUVA researchers built one of the first fully integrated self-powered wireless sensor nodes (2012).
In the 5G standardUVA wakeup-radio research led to a low-power wakeup signal in today's 5G standard.
New materials and devicesFerroelectric hafnium-zirconium-oxide memory films, oscillator-based Ising machines, and optical measurement of heat flow in nanoscale chip materials.
Photonics and THzSuperconducting THz detectors for radio astronomy, and single-photon and high-speed photodetectors from award-winning photonics groups.
AI compute and architectureLed a national center on processing-in-memory; UVA architecture tools such as the HotSpot thermal model and Rodinia GPU benchmarks are used across the field.
