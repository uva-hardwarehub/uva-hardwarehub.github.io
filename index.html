Semiconductors and AI Hardware

Hardware for AI, from atoms to the edge.

We connect UVA researchers who build the hardware AI runs on: new materials and devices, ultra-low-power circuits, wireless and photonic links, compute architectures, and trustworthy hardware/software systems. Our focus is energy-efficient, secure AI in the datacenter and at the far edge, including batteryless sensors that connect AI to the physical world.

About the hub

AI's progress is now limited by hardware: by the energy it takes to compute and move data, by the trustworthiness of the chips it runs on, and by how much of the physical world it can sense. The Semiconductors and AI Hardware Hub, one of the University of Virginia's Research Hubs, brings together researchers who work on these limits at every level of the stack.

Five research themes run from materials to systems. Three testbed themes put that hardware to work in health and wearables, in the built environment, and in autonomy, the last together with UVA's Physical AI Hub. Photonics, detector and materials work is coordinated with the Quantum Photonics, Materials and Algorithms Hub. The hub builds teams for major federal and industry programs, connects faculty with Virginia's VAST, VFAB and VMEC networks, and trains the next generation of chip designers.

Why now
  • Energy. The cost of computing and moving data now limits how far AI can scale.
  • Data. Frontier models trained on internet data. The next gains need continuous, grounded data from the physical world.
  • Trust. AI in critical systems needs hardware that is secure and verifiable from the ground up.
  • Workforce. National semiconductor priorities (CHIPS, DoD microelectronics) need chip designers.

Signature initiative

The Sensory and Computational Fabric for Physical AI

Imagine batteryless, networked nodes built into textiles, structures and rooms, powered only by ambient heat, light, motion or radio waves. Together they act as the senses of large AI models: they filter, compress and learn locally, and send grounded signal upstream.

The initiative draws on every research theme and is demonstrated in our health and built-environment testbeds, for example with networks of chiplets woven into fabric.

  • T5Distributed and federated learning across the fabric
  • T4Machine learning in memory, at nanowatts
  • T3Ultra-low-power links and wakeup radios
  • T2Nanowatt compute and energy harvesting
  • T1New devices and sensing materials

Why UVA

Two decades of leadership in the hardware that makes edge AI possible. All items to be verified before publication.

First self-powered sensor nodeUVA researchers built the first fully integrated self-powered wireless sensor node (2012).
Nanowatt computingA complete RISC-V system-on-chip running at about 3 nW, and chips that use over 10,000× less power than conventional ones.
In the 5G standardUVA wakeup-radio research led to a low-power wakeup signal in the 5G standard.
Device-to-system depthExpertise spanning materials, devices, circuits, architectures, security and ML, in one place.
